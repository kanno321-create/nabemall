The arithmetic that pays for this pack

A finished short has to land between 35 and 45 seconds. So people set the script's total cut duration to 35–45 seconds, and that is wrong, because the finished file gains 1.75 seconds the script never sees: 0.35 s of tail plus a 1.4 s end card.

Work it backwards. The measured narration has to land inside 33.25–43.25 s. The TTS gate passes anything between 0.80×Σ and Σ. To be safe wherever it lands you need Σ ≥ 41.57 and Σ ≤ 43.25 — so Σ is 42 seconds, fixed. Set it to 40 and a script that passes the script gate and passes the TTS gate produces a 33.75-second file that fails QA. Set it to 44 and you get 45.75 s and fail the other end. Both are caught after every credit has been spent.

The pack states the derivation, the valid cut combinations at Σ=42 (9 through 13 cuts — 14 is arithmetically unable to hold a hook), and the word window for each.

Three accidents specific to this genre

  • 1. A motionless celestial body is a dead video. A galaxy rotates about its core, a star's surface boils and throws flares, a planet spins while its clouds move at a different rate. A frame where only the camera moves gets swiped instantly — and the fix is not "raise the model tier", which spends double and changes nothing.
  • 2. Explanatory scenes built with i2v fail. Ask a generative model for a diagram, a formula or a decoding and it returns plausible abstract patterning. Those cuts have to be 2D motion graphics, with the lettering laid on at render time with a font — because a generation model breaks the spelling.
  • 3. Occlusion motion duplicates the subject. "The moon passes behind the planet" defeats the model's tracking and it draws a copy in front. You get two moons. The fix is cut design, not prompting; regenerating just duplicates again.

Cost control is a routing table, not a preference

Every cut declares a motion tier that maps 1:1 to an engine: static drift goes to the cheap family, orbital tracking to the fast tier, explosions and re-entry to the expensive one. The gate is kling ≥ 50%. Both directions are errors — smearing a dynamic cut on the cheap model, and rendering a static cut on the expensive one, which is the usual way a credit budget disappears.

The pack also carries the order-length rule that catches people out: a 5-second hook fits the 5-second preset only in the first cut, because the crossfade compensation attaches to the front of every clip except that one. Put the 5 anywhere else and it needs 5.35 s, lands on the 10-second preset, and that single cut costs twice as much.

This is the english-channel pack

English runs at about 2.6 words per second and is measured in words; Korean runs at about 6.4 characters per second and is measured in characters. Put an English script through review rules built for Korean and every correct script fails, because the same meaning takes roughly twice the characters and the speaking-time estimate doubles. People who run a Korean channel and then open an English one get stuck at exactly this point.
