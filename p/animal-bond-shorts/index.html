Most AI animal-video guides assume only animals are on screen. This one is about the hand.

Animal bond shorts — a rescued fox, a shelter cat, a wild neighbour that finally comes close — are one of the highest-performing emotional genres on YouTube. They are also the hardest to generate, because the moment that makes them work is a person touching an animal, and that is exactly where AI video breaks.

This is the production standard from a working channel, generalised into 12 Claude Code skills.

Built and measured on the Kling 3.0 and Seedance 2.0 families. The routing rules are written as properties, so they carry over to other AI filmmaking engines.

What breaks in this genre — and what's in the pack for it

  • Six fingers, fingers melting into fur → positive count locks, never negations
  • A different person in every shot → a second master reference for the human
  • A face that mushes into something ghostly → show it or remove it, never half-hidden
  • The hand passes through the animal → four contact-prompt rules + forced high-tier routing
  • The animal teleports into frame → frame the still at the instant BEFORE touching
  • A back view turns and becomes a stranger → i2v has no face reference; avoid the motions that break identity

There is a dedicated "contact" field: when the script declares a shot as touching, i2v force-promotes it to the expensive model. Everywhere else the routing pushes work down to the cheap tier — the majority of shots in a normal episode.
