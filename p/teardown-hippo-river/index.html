Why a whole episode instead of a prompt list

A single good prompt teaches you almost nothing, because you cannot see what it was competing with. Twenty of them in sequence show you the thing that actually matters: how the same subject is held identical across cuts, how framing is specified so the engine does not reinterpret it, and how many shots in a working episode are ordered on the cheap tier.

In this episode: 18 cuts on the low-motion engine, 2 on the higher tier. That ratio is not an accident and it is most of the budget.

Three things you can read straight off the prompts

  • Identity is locked with a positive count, never a negation. Prompts here say what the animal is — species, ear shape, coat, toe count. They do not list what it must not be, because a negation tends to summon exactly the thing it forbids.
  • Framing is stated as what fills the frame, not as a shot-size name. Shot-size vocabulary is interpreted loosely by every engine we have measured.
  • The motion prompt is split into subject, action and environment as separate lines. Collapsing them into one sentence is where drift starts.
