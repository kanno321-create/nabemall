Why this genre is worth taking apart

Space footage is mostly slow drift punctuated by a few violent beats, and getting that split right is most of the budget.

Watch how few cuts need the expensive engine. Scale is the hard part in this genre, not motion: the prompts state what fills the frame rather than naming a shot size, because shot-size words do not survive.

The routing, in this episode

Every cut ran tier 1. Tier is assigned before a cut is ordered, and it decides the engine. A static shot on the expensive engine is pure waste; a hard-motion shot on the cheap one costs more, because the re-order costs more than the correct order would have.

What the episode argues

3I/ATLAS, discovered July 1, 2025, is only the third interstellar object ever detected. Its methane-to-water ratio is about 11 times anything measured from comets born in our solar system, and ESO/JWST isotope studies suggest it formed around an ancient, metal-poor star — possibly up to 12 billion years ago, far older than the 4.6-billion-year-old Sun. SETI listened for signals and heard only silence. As of mid-2026 it is beyond Jupiter, on a hyperbolic trajectory out of the solar system, never to return.

Three things you can read straight off the prompts

  • Identity is locked with a positive count, never a negation — a forbidden thing tends to get summoned.
  • Framing is stated as what fills the frame, not as a shot-size name. Shot-size vocabulary is interpreted loosely by every engine we have measured.
  • Subject, action and environment are separate lines in the motion prompt. Collapsing them into one sentence is where drift starts.
