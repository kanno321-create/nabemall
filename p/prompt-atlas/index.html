This is not a prompt pack. It is a production corpus with an index: 83 published episodes across four genres, with every prompt filed under the job its shot had to do in the edit — hook, body, closing.

The index is the product. Two thousand prompts in a folder is a dump you will never open twice. The same prompts grouped by shot job is something you open at the exact moment you are stuck on a specific kind of cut.

What is in it

  • 01-hook.md — 90 opening prompts. The scarcest kind, because an episode only gets one.
  • 02-body.md — 1,126 prompts for the middle, where most cuts live
  • 05-closing.md — 81 closing prompts, carrying the ask
  • index.csv — every prompt as a row: episode, cut number, genre, role, framing, motion tier, duration, still prompt, and the motion block split into subject / action / environment

Four genres: wildlife documentary, space documentary, true-crime reconstruction, animal bond.

What every entry carries

Not just the text. Each prompt comes with the episode it belongs to, the cut number, the intended duration, the framing, and the engine tier it was actually routed to. You can see what a 4.5-second wide dynamic shot was asked for, next to what a 7-second low-motion close-up was asked for, in the same genre, by the same hand.

Three things the corpus shows that an article cannot

  • Identity is locked with a positive count, never a negation. The wildlife prompts name the species, the ear shape, the coat pattern, the toe count. None of them list what the animal must not be — a forbidden thing tends to get summoned.
  • Framing is written as what fills the frame, not as a shot-size name. Shot size is in the metadata because the pipeline needed it; the prompts themselves describe the picture.
  • Subject, action and environment are separate lines in every motion block. Collapsing them into one sentence is where drift starts. 983 of the entries have all three.

And one thing about money

The motion tier is recorded on every prompt. Read the distribution and you can see what a real episode costs: the majority route to the cheap engine, and the expensive tier is reserved for the handful of cuts that genuinely need it. That ratio is most of a production budget, and it is visible here as data rather than as advice.

Why this cannot be assembled by someone else

Every prompt in here was written to order a specific cut of a specific episode, and that episode exists. You cannot generate this corpus; you have to have run channels for months and kept the build folders. That is the whole moat, and it is why the entries are messy in places — they are working files, not a curated showcase.
